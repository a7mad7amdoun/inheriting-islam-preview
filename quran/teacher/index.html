The record

Four cities, four communities, the same thing needed in each.

Hamza Abdul-Tawwaab Former imam · teacher · builder

Hamza’s work began with teaching Qur’an and leading tarāwīḥ. Inheriting Qur’an continues that work through live lessons, with time to hear each student read.

He has taught at Islah LA in Los Angeles, Midtown Mosque in Memphis, Masjid As-Sabur in Las Vegas, and Atlanta Masjid in Atlanta. His roots are in Los Angeles; he is currently based in Morocco, and the classes run on American clocks.

He holds a shahādah in memorization of the Qur'an, and is currently reading the Qur'an to a teacher for an ijāzah in recitation.

A reflection from the house

The manner of teaching matters alongside the material. Learners should be able to ask, pause and try again.

Context & a step to carry with you

The verse addresses the manner of inviting others to Allah. It also calls for discussion in the best manner and affirms that Allah knows those who stray and those who are guided.

One step to carry with you

Bring one question about how you learn best to your first conversation.

How he teaches

Four things that do not change, whoever is in the room.

  1. 01

    The mouth before the chart

    Time goes on where a letter is made, not on memorizing a table of letters. A student who knows where the ḍād lives can find it again; a student who memorized a chart has to start over every time.

  2. 02

    Out loud, every session, by name

    You will read aloud every week. This is the entire reason the class is small and the entire reason it is live. A teacher can hear what silent practice cannot reveal.

  3. 03

    Corrected immediately, never mocked

    You get stopped on the spot, told exactly what your tongue did, and asked to do it again. Nobody is laughed at, nobody is compared to anyone else, and nobody's age is ever a joke.

  4. 04

    Told the truth about your progress

    If you are not practicing, you will be told kindly and directly. If you are moving faster than the cohort, you will be told that too, and moved.

Scope, stated up front

What this program is not.

The program teaches reading, tajwīd, and memorizing Juz ʿAmma. These boundaries help you decide whether it fits what you want to learn.

It is not full-muṣḥaf ḥifẓ

Memorizing Juz ʿAmma is part of what this program does. Memorizing the whole muṣḥaf is not — that wants a ḥifẓ circle with daily revision, and a good one is usually in a building near you rather than on a screen.

It is not an ijāzah track

This does not lead to a license or a chain of transmission. It leads to you reading Juz ʿAmma correctly. That is a different, smaller, and for most adults far more urgent thing.

It is not Arabic as a language

You will learn to read the script and pronounce it properly. You will not come out speaking Arabic or parsing grammar — that is a separate study, and pretending otherwise would waste your year.

And if you are past a stage, you will be moved past it

If the placement call shows you already read fluently, you will not be sat through the letters — you will be placed against tajwīd or memorization instead. And if what you actually need is something this program genuinely does not teach, you will be told that plainly and pointed to teachers who are not us.

A student paying every month for a class they have outgrown is not a business win; it is somebody being quietly taken.

Recommendations

You read, he listens, and you get told honestly where you are and how far the walk is. There is no charge for that and nothing follows it unless you want it to.
