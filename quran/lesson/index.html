01.2 / Your first lesson

A letter.
A beginning.

Meet the Arabic letters, see how their shapes change, and explore short vowels and the places where sounds are formed. Read at your own pace.

Audio is not available yet. You can explore the letters and explanations below. A teacher can demonstrate the sounds and listen to your reading.

One letter at a time

Choose a letter to see its name and a written explanation. This page does not listen to or grade your pronunciation.

Closing clause of the verse

Meaning in brief · Original English paraphraseThe Prophet ﷺ is instructed to recite the Qur’an with measured care.

A reflection from the house

Letters become familiar through careful practice. Give the shape, sound and connection of each one your attention.

Context & a step to carry with you

The opening of this surah addresses the Prophet ﷺ about standing in prayer at night. This excerpt is the instruction on recitation at the end of verse 4.

One step to carry with you

Practice one letter, then ask a teacher to hear your pronunciation.

Part one

Twenty-eight letters, by name and by sound.

Tap any letter to read its name and where in the mouth it is made. The written descriptions are a starting point; a teacher can demonstrate the sounds and hear your practice.

ا

alifā

A carrier rather than a sound of its own — it lengthens the fatḥah before it. The hamzah ء it often sits under is made at the deepest part of the throat.

The empty space

Explore a letter to read its name and how its sound is formed. The written explanation supports practice with a teacher.

Part two

One letter, four shapes.

This is the lesson that turns shapes back into reading. The letter that looks unfamiliar in the middle of a word is the same letter you already learned — it is just holding hands on both sides.

بAlonebāʾ
بـOpeningba-
ـبـMiddle-ba-
ـبClosing-b

Six letters break this rule — ا د ذ ر ز و never join to the letter after them, which is why words seem to have gaps in the middle of them. That single fact removes most of the confusion people carry for years.

Where most self-teaching breaks

An adult who learned the twenty-eight letters from an app or a chart usually stalls exactly here. The letters were learned in their alone shape, and then a real word arrives with none of them in it.

Recognizing joined letters takes practice. A teacher can help you notice the patterns and work through the shapes that still feel unfamiliar. There is no fixed timetable.

Part three

Then the three marks that make it speech.

Three small marks, and the letters start to sound like the words you have been standing in prayer with your whole life.

The short vowels

بَFatḥahba
بِKasrahbi
بُḌammahbu

And then the long ones

بَاAlifbā
بِيYāʾbī
بُوWāwbū

Each long vowel is its short vowel held for twice the time — not a different sound, a longer one. Holding it correctly is already the beginning of tajwīd, which is why the two are not really separate subjects.

The part that needs a teacher

Where the sounds are formed.

The classical scholars mapped seventeen exact points of articulation inside five zones. You do not need the seventeen to begin — you need to know that a letter has a place, and that yours is either on it or near it.

The throat

الحَلْق

Some of these sounds may be unfamiliar to an English speaker. A teacher can help you distinguish ح from ه and practice ع clearly.

Six letters · three depths
ءهعحغخ

The tongue

اللِّسان

Ten of the seventeen points and eighteen of the letters — the deepest part against the soft palate, the middle against the roof, the edges against the molars, and the tip against the teeth and gums. Most of the corrections in a class happen inside this zone.

Eighteen letters · ten points
قكجشيض لنرطدت صزسظذث

The two lips

الشَّفَتان

The easiest zone to reach and the easiest to be lazy in. و needs the lips genuinely rounded and not touching; م needs them fully closed, or the sound that should run through the nose does not.

Four letters
فوبم

The empty space

الجَوْف

Not a contact point at all — the open cavity of the mouth and throat, where the three long vowels live. و and ي appear here as well as above, because each is a consonant in one place and a lengthening in another. That is not a contradiction; it is the same letter doing two jobs.

Three long vowels
اوي

The nose

الخَيْشُوم

A zone with no letters of its own. It is where the ghunnah is made — the held nasal sound in نّ and مّ, and in half the rules of tajwīd later on. Pinch your nose and say it; if nothing changes, it is not there.

No letters · one sound
نّمّ

From the Prophetic teachings

Prophet Muhammad ﷺ

Sahih · Authentic narration

وَأَنَّ أَحَبَّ الأَعْمَالِ أَدْوَمُهَا إِلَى اللَّهِ، وَإِنْ قَلَّ

Sahih al-Bukhari 6464 ↗

Narrated by Aisha

Excerpt · Closing clause on consistency

Meaning in brief · Our paraphraseDeeds that continue steadily are most beloved to Allah, even when they are small.

A step to carry into your day

Choose a short review you can return to: a letter, a sound, or a line your teacher has checked. Let steadiness matter more than a streak.

Context & source

Aisha narrates this teaching in the chapter on moderation and regular deeds. The complete saying also calls for sound, balanced effort and reminds us that deeds alone do not secure entry to Paradise.

The Arabic is from the linked narration. Our paraphrase and practical reflection are editorial writing, pending review; they are not part of the hadith text.

What the live hour actually is

Practice with a teacher who hears you.

Everything above can be studied from this page for free, and you are welcome to. What a page cannot do is notice that your ḍād is coming out as a dāl, tell you which edge of the tongue to move, and make you say it eleven more times until it lands.

So a session looks like this

After a short introduction, students take turns reading while the teacher listens and offers corrections. Adult students can review the recording while enrolled. Children’s sessions are never recorded.
