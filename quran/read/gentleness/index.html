Room to try again.

Read a concise prophetic saying about gentleness, then think about the way we give and receive correction.

01 / Source

Begin with the source.

Sahih Muslim 2594a

From Prophet Muhammad ﷺ

Sahih · Authentic narration

إِنَّ الرِّفْقَ لاَ يَكُونُ فِي شَىْءٍ إِلاَّ زَانَهُ وَلاَ يُنْزَعُ مِنْ شَىْءٍ إِلاَّ شَانَهُ

English translation · Prophetic saying

Verily, lenience is not found in anything but it adorns it, and it is not removed from anything but it makes it defective.

Translation published by HadeethEnc ↗

Original English paraphrase · Inheriting Islam

The Prophet Muhammad ﷺ teaches that gentleness makes things beautiful, while its absence leaves them marred.

Read the complete source ↗
Text and source notes

Complete prophetic saying; transmission chain omitted.

Narrated by Aisha, wife of the Prophet ﷺ.

Aisha narrates this saying in the chapter on the virtue of gentleness. This record uses narration 2594a alone; it does not combine details from neighboring narrations.

The English is our summary of meaning, not a literal translation. Source checked 2026-10-16.

Understanding the source

Source context, not a separate scholarly commentary

Stay close to the narration

Aisha narrates this saying from the Prophet Muhammad ﷺ. The source places it in the chapter on the virtue of gentleness, within the Book of Virtue, Good Manners and Maintaining Family Ties.

This entry gives the saying without a teaching-classroom incident. We do not attach a scene from another narration or claim it was spoken about Qur’an lessons. The application below is our proposed reflection and remains subject to editorial review.

Sahih Muslim 2594a · The Virtue of Gentleness, via Sunnah.com ↗

02 / Reflection

Bring it into the everyday.

Our reflection · Editorial draft

A learner may need a correction and encouragement at the same moment. They may have practiced a sound carefully and still be unable to produce it. The next response can help them hear what needs to change and give them enough room to try.

For this learning house, gentleness can become a concrete teaching habit: offer one clear correction, model it, then listen to the next attempt. An explanation that overwhelms a beginner may need to be broken into smaller steps. The goal is understanding, not winning an exchange.

Being gentle does not require pretending that every attempt is accurate. A teacher can say that a sound needs work and explain why. A student can ask for a slower example or admit that the explanation has not yet made sense. Clarity and care can belong in the same sentence.

We should also notice who is listening. A private correction may be easier to receive than an unexpected public one. Asking how someone prefers feedback can prevent avoidable embarrassment. These suggestions describe a learning practice; the hadith itself is not a checklist of classroom techniques.

For someone returning after a long break, the first useful step may be small. Open the lesson, find the last sound you understood and bring one question to a teacher. A page can offer practice, but it cannot hear and assess your recitation.

One optional practice

Make the next correction usable

If you are helping someone practice, ask whether they would like feedback. Choose one point, demonstrate it clearly, and leave time for another attempt. If you are learning, ask for one example you can practice before moving on.

A suggested action, not a prescribed religious obligation.

03 / Next step

Take the context with you.

Share the complete reference with someone you learn alongside. Discuss how you each prefer to receive feedback. Keep the conversation about your own needs rather than evaluating a person who is absent.

فَبِمَا رَحۡمَةٖ مِّنَ ٱللَّهِ لِنتَ لَهُمۡۖ

Translation of the meanings · Excerpt · 3:159

So by mercy from Allah, [O Muhammad], you were lenient with them.

Saheeh International ↗ · via Al Quran Cloud

Opening clause of the verse

Meaning in brief · Original English paraphraseBy Allah’s mercy, the Prophet ﷺ was gentle with them.

A reflection from the house

Read this Qur’anic passage alongside the separate hadith about gentleness. Our reflection is to make feedback clear enough to use and kind enough to receive.

Context & a step to carry with you

The full verse addresses the Prophet’s ﷺ gentleness toward his companions, and continues with forgiveness, consultation and reliance on Allah. The excerpt is not the whole instruction.

One step to carry with you

Ask before correcting someone, and leave room for another attempt.
