01 / Source

Begin with the source.

Sahih al-Bukhari 3

Original English summary of part of the narration · Inheriting Islam

After receiving revelation in the cave of Hira, the Prophet Muhammad ﷺ returned to Khadijah in distress. She reassured him by recalling his care for relatives, vulnerable people and guests, and his help for those facing hardship. She then accompanied him to Waraqah ibn Nawfal.

Narrated by Aisha, mother of the believers.

Understanding the source

Source context and original narrative summary

An account, with its boundaries

The Prophet ﷺ tells Khadijah what happened after his fear eased. Her reassurance recalls his care for others. She then accompanies him to Waraqah, who listens and responds.

This page concentrates on her response, within the larger account of the first revelation. It adds no imagined dialogue, thoughts or gestures. Read the complete report for its sequence and wording.

02 / Reflection

Bring it into the everyday.

Our reflection · Editorial draft

Khadijah is not a background detail in this account. She listens, speaks and acts. Reading carefully allows us to notice her contribution without decorating it with a scene the source does not give us. Her response deserves attention in its own right.

There is something to consider here about the quality of our attention to others. Reassurance can be more personal when it remembers something true about a person: a responsibility they carried, care they showed or help they gave. It need not become a sweeping promise about what will happen next.

For our own ordinary conversations, we propose beginning by listening before deciding what someone needs to hear. A person may want company, practical help, or help finding someone with the right knowledge. Asking can be more useful than assuming. This is our reflection, not a claim that every situation parallels the first revelation.

The distinction matters. Prophetic revelation is not an analogy for every difficult experience. We can honor the particular account, name the people who appear in it, and still reflect carefully on how we respond to someone who comes to us unsettled.

When retelling a meaningful story, it is tempting to make it more vivid. Here, keeping the reference close is part of the care. If we do not know a detail, we can leave it unsaid. The recorded account gives us enough to return to and discuss.

One optional practice

Offer attention, then ask

If someone comes to you with a worry, give them time to speak. Ask whether they want a listening ear or help with a next step. Mention something true you appreciate about them, without promising an outcome you cannot know.

A suggested action, not a prescribed religious obligation.

03 / Next step

Take the context with you.

Read the complete narration before sharing the story. Discuss what Khadijah does in the account, and identify which details come from the source. Keep your own reflections clearly separate from the narration.

A reflection from the house

This address to the Prophet ﷺ invites careful reading alongside the account of Khadijah. Keep each source distinct, and avoid promising outcomes for another person’s life.

Context & a step to carry with you

This address is specifically to the Prophet ﷺ. It is a separate Qur’anic passage, not part of the narration about Khadijah’s reassurance at the beginning of revelation.
