Make time to read.

The shape of it

What "live and recorded" means here, precisely.

01

The live hour

Online, once a week, at a fixed time. A short piece of new material, then students reading aloud one at a time while the rest follow. The live session gives the teacher time to hear your reading and help with corrections.

02

The recording

Every adult session is recorded and yours for as long as you are enrolled. It is there for the week you are traveling, the week the baby is up all night, and the week you simply did not get it the first time. Children's classes are never recorded.

03

The week in between

Set aside a manageable time to practice aloud between classes. Note the letters or passages you find difficult, and bring those questions to the next session.

Why the cohort is kept small

If a class is large enough that you can hide in it, it is too large to teach reading in. The number is set by how many people can each read out loud in an hour and still be corrected properly — not by how many seats we would like to sell.

Why you start together and finish together

Adults learning to read do better beside other adults learning to read, which is the same reason children are taught apart from them. Rolling enrollment quietly turns every session into a beginner's session for one person and a repeat for everyone else, so we do not do it.

The hours are genuinely undecided, and that is on purpose. Students are spread across US time zones and the teacher is currently based in Morocco, so there is one hour a week that either works for a nurse in Atlanta and a father in Los Angeles, or does not. Picking it before knowing who is coming would be guessing.

So the waitlist below is not a mailing list. It is the actual input: tell us when you could genuinely make a class, and the hour gets set from the answers.

What being on the waitlist commits you to

Nothing. No card, no deposit, no place held against your name. When an hour is set you get told, and you decide then.

If the hour that gets chosen does not work for you, say so — that is a useful answer too, and it is how the second cohort gets its time.

Excerpt: the two clauses about cooperation

Meaning in brief · Original English paraphraseHelp one another in goodness and mindfulness of Allah; do not help one another in wrongdoing or aggression.

A reflection from the house

A class is a shared responsibility: arriving ready, making room for another voice, and giving each learner time.

Context & a step to carry with you

The full verse concerns sacred rites, pilgrimage and the duty not to let hostility lead to transgression. Its call to cooperate in good is paired with a prohibition on cooperating in harm.

Before you ask

The questions everybody asks.

Is it online, or do I have to be somewhere?

Online. The live session is a video call, so a cohort can hold a student in Atlanta, one in Memphis and one in Las Vegas at the same time. If a masjid wants to gather people in one room and join together, that works too — that is what hosting a cohort is.

What do I need to have?

A phone, tablet or computer with a working microphone, and somewhere you can read out loud without feeling watched. That is the real requirement — you will be speaking, not typing.

The four class books are free PDFs. Your teacher will explain which one to begin with and whether another edition would help.

What happens if I miss a week?

Adult students can review the session recording while enrolled. Children’s classes are never recorded; a parent should contact the teacher about missed work. If life gets in the way of regular attendance, tell us so we can discuss the next step.

Can my children join?

Yes — in a children's class, run separately from the adults' with its own hours and its own pacing. Children's classes are for ages 6 to 12, boys and girls together.

A parent or guardian stays within earshot for every class, the teacher speaks only to parents outside class, and children's classes are never recorded. A parent signs a consent form before a short placement call. Teenagers will have their own classes later, never mixed in with adults. Use the availability form and say how old they are.

How long before I can read?

We are not going to give you a number, and you should be careful with anyone who does. It depends on where you start, how often you practice out loud, and whether Arabic script is completely new to your eye.

What we will tell you honestly, in the first ten minutes of a call, is whether the distance in front of you is a few months or a year — after hearing you read.

Can I be taught one to one instead?

Yes, and there are three private seats at a time — $320 a month for a standing weekly session, or $90 for a single one.

It is the same teacher, the same syllabus and the same correction as the fifty-dollar cohort. What you are paying for is a weekly 45-minute session with the teacher, and if a cohort would serve you just as well we will tell you so rather than take the difference. The reasoning, in full.

Are men and women taught together?

No. Adult classes are separate for men and women, each with its own hour. Children aged 6 to 12 share a class.

Do I pay for the whole thing up front?

No. Fifty dollars a month, per student, while you are enrolled. No enrollment fee, no term contract, and no upsell waiting three lessons in. Stop whenever you need to and start again when you can.

What if I cannot afford fifty dollars?

Then say so, and you will be taught anyway. There are sponsored seats, reduced rates, and masajid who cover students from their own community.

There is a box on the form for exactly this so that nobody has to write a paragraph explaining their finances to a stranger. Tick it and it comes up on the call.
