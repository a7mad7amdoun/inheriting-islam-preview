Excerpt: the two clauses about cooperation

Meaning in brief · Original English paraphraseHelp one another in goodness and mindfulness of Allah; do not help one another in wrongdoing or aggression.

A reflection from the house

A welcoming class needs practical care as well as a place to meet. Consider language, access and the needs of people arriving for the first time.

Context & a step to carry with you

The full verse concerns sacred rites, pilgrimage and the duty not to let hostility lead to transgression. Its call to cooperate in good is paired with a prohibition on cooperating in harm.

The arrangement

Who does what, in full.

What the masjid does

  • Announces it — from the minbar, in the newsletter, in the group chat. Coming from you, it is an invitation; coming from us, it is an advertisement. The adults' cohort and the children's are announced separately; they reach different people.
  • Gives us one point of contact — a person who can coordinate questions and announcements.
  • Tells us honestly what the community needs — who is asking, what has been tried before, and what did not work.
  • Optionally sponsors seats — many masajid cover a handful of students who would not otherwise enroll. Entirely optional, and never a condition.

What we do

  • Teach it — every session, by a former imam, from the letters through to Juz ʿAmma with tajwīd.
  • Place every student — a short call each, out loud, so nobody sits in the wrong class and quietly leaves. Children are placed with a parent on the call.
  • Record adult sessions — recordings are available to adult students while enrolled. Children's classes are never recorded.
  • Handle the admin — sign-ups, reminders, payments and the awkward money conversations, so that none of it lands on your office.
  • Tell you how it is going — plainly, including if turnout is poor. You will not have to ask twice.

What it costs the masjid

Nothing. There is no hosting fee, no minimum number of students, and no revenue share asked of you. Students pay $50 a month directly, and a masjid that wants to sponsor seats may — that is the only money that would ever move between us.

What we will not do with your community

We will not take your membership list, add anyone to a mailing list they did not ask for, sell or share anyone's details, or use your name to sell website work.

How it goes

From a phone call to a class reading out loud.

No paperwork, no MOU, no board presentation unless your board wants one — in which case the one-page summary below is written for exactly that.

  1. Step one

    A conversation, twenty minutes

    Who in your community is asking, roughly how many, whether it is adults or children or both, and what has been tried before. Usually with the imam or whoever runs education. If it is not a fit, we will say so on that call rather than three weeks later.

  2. Step two

    You announce it, we write nothing for you to sign

    We give you the wording, a link, and a date. You announce it the way your community actually hears things — which is almost never a poster.

  3. Step three

    Everyone who signs up gets placed

    Each student has a short reading call so we can suggest an appropriate starting point. Children attend with a parent.

  4. Step four

    The cohort starts, small

    Weekly, at an hour that suits the people who actually enrolled. Adult sessions are recorded; children's are not. If your community would rather gather in the building and join together on one screen, that works and several will prefer it.

  5. After

    You get told the truth about how it went

    How many finished, how many can read who could not, and what we would do differently. Including if the answer is that it did not work — that is the report that makes the next one worth running.

For your board

One page you can forward without explaining it.

Everything on this page, on a single sheet: what it is, who teaches it, what it costs the masjid, what it costs a student, and what we ask for. Written to be read by somebody who has not met us and printed for somebody who does not read email.

If your board needs more than a page

Hamza will get on a call with them. He has stood in front of congregations in four cities, and a board asking hard questions about who is teaching their community is a board doing its job properly.

The questions we are asked most — who is teaching, what it costs, what happens to our members' details — are answered on the teacher's page and below.

What boards ask

The questions, answered plainly.

Who is actually teaching our community?

Hamza Abdul-Tawwaab — a former imam who taught Qur'an and led taraweeh before he built anything, and who has taught at Islah LA in Los Angeles, Midtown Mosque in Memphis, Masjid As-Sabur in Las Vegas and Atlanta Masjid in Atlanta. His teaching background, in full — including what he is not qualified to teach.

What does this cost the masjid?

Nothing. No hosting fee, no minimum, no revenue share. Students pay $50 a month directly. If the masjid chooses to sponsor seats for families who cannot carry that, it may — and that is the only money that would ever move between us.

Does it have to be online?

The teaching is delivered online, because that is what lets one teacher serve four cities. But a masjid can absolutely gather people in a room and join together on one screen, and for elders in particular that tends to work far better than asking each person to manage a video call alone.

What happens to our members' information?

Students give us their own details when they enroll, and that is all we ever hold. We do not ask for your membership list, we do not add anyone to anything they did not ask for, and we do not sell or share data — that is a written rule for this whole house, not a policy for this page.

Our website carries no trackers, no cookies and no third-party scripts. You can check.

Is this only for adults?

No. There is a cohort for adults and a cohort for children, and they are run separately — different hours, different pacing, and different teaching entirely. An adult who was never taught does not want to learn beside nine-year-olds, and nine-year-olds do not learn well in a room built for adults.

Most masajid start with one and add the other once the first is running. Tell us on the call which your community is actually asking for.

What is taught, beyond reading?

The reading track runs from the letters to Juz ʿAmma read correctly. Beyond that there is tajwīd taught as its own study, and memorization of Juz ʿAmma — the sūrahs your community already stands in prayer with, carried properly. It is not a full-muṣḥaf ḥifẓ program and it does not lead to an ijāzah, and we will say so plainly to anyone hoping otherwise.

What if only five people sign up?

Then five people learn to read. Small is the design, not the failure case — a cohort that size is one where every student reads out loud every week. If nobody at all signs up, we will tell you that too, and it costs you nothing to have asked.

Are you going to try to sell us a website?

No. Inheriting Islam does not sell website work, and a class for your community is not a way into a sales conversation about anything else.
