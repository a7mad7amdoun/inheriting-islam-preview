01.1 / Your starting point

you are.

A verse for this chapter

A beginning can be gentle.

Qur’an 94:5 · Ash-Sharh ↗

فَإِنَّ مَعَ ٱلۡعُسۡرِ يُسۡرًا

Complete verse · Verse-number ornament omitted

Meaning in brief · Original English paraphraseWith hardship there is ease.

A reflection from the house

Starting again can feel difficult. Let this be a place to name what you need, without comparing your pace with someone else’s.

Context & a step to carry with you

The surah addresses the Prophet, recalling Allah’s favors and affirming ease alongside hardship. It closes with an invitation to effort and turning toward the Lord.

One step to carry with you

Notice one part you can already do and one question to bring to a teacher.

Step 1 of 5

One

Three letters, on their own.

ب   ج   م

Can you say the sound each of these makes — not the name, the sound?

Two

Letters joined into words.

مَسْجِد    كِتَاب

Two ordinary words. Can you read them out loud from the page?

Three

A familiar line, read from the text.

الْحَمْدُ لِلَّهِ رَبِّ الْعَالَمِينَ

Read the written words if you can. Recognizing a familiar recitation from memory is different from reading its letters, and either starting point is welcome.

Four

Two āyāt to read from the page.

وَاللَّيْلِ إِذَا يَغْشَىٰ  ·  وَالنَّهَارِ إِذَا تَجَلَّىٰ

Sūrat al-Layl, the opening two. Memory cannot carry you here — can you read them without getting stuck?

Five

Now the part that is not reading.

أَنْعَمْتَ عَلَيْهِمْ

Look at the نْ in the first word. Do you know whether it is pronounced clearly, hidden, merged into the next letter, or flipped to a mīm — and why? Answering yes does not end the road; it moves you onto a different one.

The five places people start

Every one of these is a normal place to begin.

Where you start

Lesson one, the letters.

Begin with the twenty-eight letters, their sounds, and where those sounds are made. You may already recognize familiar recitations by ear, or the script may be entirely new. Either way, the first lesson gives you a place to begin.

Where you start

Lesson one briefly, then joining.

You know the letters standing alone, which is further than you think. What is missing is the thing that turns letters into reading: a letter changes shape depending on where it sits in a word, and until that clicks, a familiar letter in an unfamiliar position looks like a stranger.

Roughly where this lands you: the back half of stage 01 and into stage 02.

Where you start

Finishing the qāʿidah, then reading.

You can decode words, and a full line is still too much at once. That usually means one of the marks is not automatic yet — sukūn, shaddah, tanwīn or the long vowels — so every few words you have to stop and work it out. The fix is not more effort, it is the last third of the qāʿidah, and then reading out loud with someone correcting you.

Roughly where this lands you: the end of stage 02, moving into stage 03.

Where you start

Reading fluency.

You read what you know and slow down on what you do not, which means the decoding works and the fluency has not been built yet. That is a practice problem, not a knowledge problem, and it is the fastest stage to move through — it needs unfamiliar text, out loud, every week, with someone stopping you at the right moments.

Roughly where this lands you: stage 03, and stage 04 sooner than most.

Where you start

Juz ʿAmma, with tajwīd.

You can read. What you have not been taught is what the marks oblige you to do — and that last step is the one most adults never get, because it is the point at which most classes assume you learned it as a child.

For the record, the answer to step five is that the nūn is pronounced clearly, because the letter after it is made in the throat. That is one of four things that can happen to that nūn, and knowing which is which is most of what stage 04 is.

Roughly where this lands you: stage 04 — the thirtieth juz, read properly.

Where you start

Past the reading. Which is where the other half begins.

Your answers suggest that tajwīd or memorization may be a useful next step. A teacher will listen to your reading on the placement call and confirm whether there are any foundations to revisit.

Reading Juz ʿAmma well and carrying it in your chest are two different things. So is knowing a rule and applying it without thinking at speed. Both of those are their own work, and neither of them is what the qāʿidah was ever for.

  • Tajwīd properly, as its own study — the rules learned and drilled rather than recited about, with someone listening to whether you actually apply them
  • Memorizing Juz ʿAmma — the thirtieth juz committed properly, with the tajwīd holding while you recite from memory, which is where most people's falls apart

Both are being formed now alongside the reading cohorts. Say on the form that you read already and you will be placed against those rather than against the letters — and if after a call the honest answer is that you need a ḥifẓ circle for the whole muṣḥaf or a teacher working toward an ijāzah, you will be told that and pointed there instead.

Ask about tajwīd and memorization Bring this to your masjid

What this is not

A teacher confirms your starting point.

This gets you in the right neighborhood, and that is all it is built to do. Two people who stop at the same line can need completely different lessons — one has never seen the letters, the other learned them at nine and lost them at nineteen.

So the real placement happens on a call, out loud, in about ten minutes — you read, Hamza listens, and the starting point comes from your mouth rather than from a page. There is no charge for that and no obligation after it.
