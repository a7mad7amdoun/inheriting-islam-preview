01.3 / The reading desk

Open a book.
Keep learning.

A small library for learning to read, practicing tajwīd, and memorizing Juz ʿAmma. Free draft editions to download, print, and share with your students.

Three books and a companion

Find the book for your next step.

They are meant to be read in sequence — the tajwīd book assumes you can already decode a line, and the ḥifẓ book assumes you can already recite one correctly. Each carries the same certificate standard as the stage it belongs to.

Free draft editions

Written by Hamza Abdul-Tawwaab. These books have been checked, but have not yet been taught through with a cohort. Keep the author’s name when sharing, and send corrections to Hamza.

01 · Begin here · no reading prerequisite

The Inheriting Qāʿidah

For your first steps in reading Arabic. Open lesson one and practise its letters before moving on to joining and fluency.

How to practise with this material

Work through one row at a time. Identify each letter before reading the practice words; these are invented drills, not Qur’anic verses. Mark an unfamiliar shape and ask Hamza to check it before moving on.

02 · Once you can decode a line

Tajwīd for Juz ʿAmma

For readers who can already decode a line. Preview one rule and practise it with the examples from Juz ʿAmma.

How to practise with this material

Choose one rule and find its marks in the examples. Read a short passage with that rule in mind, then ask Hamza to hear it. The book uses the marks in the muṣḥaf rather than relying on color coding.

Carrying Juz ʿAmma

For learners who can recite correctly and want a steady memorization routine. Begin with the opening lesson and choose a manageable daily portion.

Read the opening guidance, then agree a manageable portion with your teacher. Keep revision beside new memorization. The final source notes distinguish established practice, research, and the author’s judgment.

The Juz ʿAmma Tracker

For learners revising Juz ʿAmma. Preview the three sheets, then print the weekly sheet and mark your first revision session.

Print the sheet that fits your routine and keep it beside your muṣḥaf. Record what you actually revised; leave unfinished work for your next session. Use a fresh weekly sheet when you begin a new week.

Why these books were written

We wanted materials written for this teaching sequence that families and teachers could print and share freely. Hamza wrote these books for that purpose, with the permissions and draft status stated clearly.

So the syllabus was written out instead. What is here is not a summary of the class — it is the class, on paper, in the order it is taught, with the same gate at the end of each lesson that the teacher listens for.

How they were checked

How the books are reviewed.

01

Arabic checked against its source

Qur’anic words are checked against the source text. Any deliberate presentation change is explained in the book.

How this is checked

No Qurʾānic word is written into these books by hand. Every one is referenced by sūrah, āyah and word, and pulled from the Uthmani text at build time — so what is printed is byte for byte what the source says, or the build stops. The qāʿidah drops one mark and says on its own pages which one and why; nothing else is altered anywhere.

02

Each lesson builds on the last

Exercises are checked so students encounter the marks and forms already introduced in their lessons.

How this is checked

A drill in lesson six cannot contain a mark taught in lesson nine. That rule is code, not care — the first time it ran it found six places where the book was quietly asking a student to read something it had not taught yet.

03

Files checked for clear Arabic

The PDFs are checked for the intended embedded fonts, so Arabic characters can display as designed.

How this is checked

Arabic that silently falls back to a system font still looks correct — that is what makes it dangerous. Every character is checked against the fonts actually embedded, and the finished file is checked again for any face that should not be in it.

Free, and that is not a launch offer

These stay free. Print them at home, run them off at the masjid, hand them to a student who will never enroll here. If you teach from them and they help, that is the whole return — the ajr for it is worth more to this house than a price would be.

Attribution is asked for, not enforced: leave the author's name on them.

These are drafts, and say so on the cover. They have been checked mechanically against the text and read end to end, but no cohort has been taught from them yet — and a teaching book is only really proven by a room full of students failing to understand page nine.

If you find an error, in the Arabic or in the teaching, write to Hamza and it will be fixed in the next build. Corrections are wanted, not merely tolerated.

A book cannot hear you read.

That is the one thing on this page that has to be said plainly. These will teach you the shapes, the rules and the method — but nothing on paper can tell you that your ḍād is actually a dāl. For that you need somebody listening, which is what the class is.

Arabic text: Verse excerpt: the closing supplication.

The full verse exalts Allah as the true King and addresses the Prophet ﷺ about receiving revelation without haste. It ends with this supplication for knowledge.
