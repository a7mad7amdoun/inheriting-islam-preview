How the reading path unfolds

+
  1. 01

    Letters & sounds

    Arabic letters, where their sounds are made, and the short vowels. Read aloud, with a teacher’s correction.

  2. 02

    Joining & reading words

    Connecting letters, long vowels, sukūn, shaddah, and tanwīn. Practice until the combinations become familiar.

  3. 03

    Reading fluency

    Read āyāt with attention to the basics of where to stop and begin again.

  4. 04

    Juz ʿAmma & tajwīd

    Apply the rules of recitation to the thirtieth part of the Qur’an, through guided practice.

This reading track leads to Juz ʿAmma. Further tajwīd and Juz ʿAmma memorization options are explained under classes. These are not full-muṣḥaf ḥifẓ or ijāzah programs.

After the reading track: tajwīd & memorization

+

After the reading

Finishing the reading track does not finish the work. Two further tracks run from there, and a student who already reads well starts in one of them rather than at the letters.

Tajwīd, as its own study

The rules of recitation taught properly and drilled — not recited about, but applied, with somebody listening for whether you actually do it when you are not thinking about it. A basic course to begin with; a fuller one for those who want to go further.

For now it is taught inside each class's recitation. Free public tajwīd sessions are planned, with their recordings shared with students. Either way it is included in the same fifty dollars.

Memorizing Juz ʿAmma

Work toward memorizing the thirtieth part of the Qur’an, with attention to accurate recitation and regular revision.

Reading from memory brings its own challenges. Recitation and memorization are practiced together, so the teacher can help with both.

Neither of these is a full-muṣḥaf ḥifẓ program and neither leads to an ijāzah. If that is what you want, say so and you will be pointed to someone who does it properly.

Certificates: what they recognize

Each completed stage is recognized with a certificate in Arabic and English. It records what the student demonstrated before the teacher. No certificates have been issued yet.

Each one says what was actually demonstrated

Not "attended sixteen sessions". The wording names the thing you did in front of a teacher — read unseen text, applied the rules through a whole juz, recited it from memory. That is the only reason a certificate is worth anything.

Every one carries a number that can be checked at the register, and states plainly that it is not an ijāzah and confers no license to transmit.

Shown blank, on purpose. These are unfilled specimens and there is no download — a certificate you can download is a certificate anyone can put their own name on.

And none have been awarded yet, because the first cohorts are still forming. The register is empty and says so.
