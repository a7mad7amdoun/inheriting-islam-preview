Hamza’s vision

The inheritance
in an ordinary life.

Meet Hamza & Khadija ↗

Faith is received from people, lived in ordinary days, and passed to someone else. Hamza wants to make room for Muslims to tell how that happens in their own lives.

A parent, a teacher, an elder, someone who came to Islam later in life. Each brings a particular experience, with something another person may recognize or learn from.

These are personal accounts. We listen with care, keeping lived experience distinct from religious teaching.

About the surah & verse numbering

A surah is a chapter of the Qur’an; an ayah is a numbered verse. These totals follow the Kufan numbering used by Quran.com. Other traditional verse-counting systems exist.

ٱلَّذِينَ يَسۡتَمِعُونَ ٱلۡقَوۡلَ فَيَتَّبِعُونَ أَحۡسَنَهُۥٓۚ أُوْلَٰٓئِكَ ٱلَّذِينَ هَدَىٰهُمُ ٱللَّهُۖ وَأُوْلَٰٓئِكَ هُمۡ أُوْلُواْ ٱلۡأَلۡبَٰبِ

Complete verse

Meaning in brief · Original English paraphraseThose who attend to what is said and follow what is best are described as guided by Allah and possessing understanding.

A reflection from the house

A personal story can open understanding. Listen carefully, while distinguishing lived experience from a claim about religious teaching.

Context & a step to carry with you

The preceding verse gives good news to servants who turn to Allah and avoid false worship. Listening here belongs with discernment and following good, not accepting every claim.

One step to carry with you

Keep one question from a conversation and look for a reliable source to explore it.

Editorial reflection · Review pending

The shape of a conversation

Three questions.
A life behind the answers.

  1. 01 / Your beginnings

    What was handed to you?

    Who first helped you understand your faith: a parent, a teacher, a friend, or a community you found later?

  2. 02 / Everyday faith

    How do you carry it now?

    The ordinary week, the difficult choices, and the practices that keep you connected.

  3. 03 / The next generation

    What are you handing forward?

    To children, students, friends, or a community. What do you hope stays with them?

A place in the conversation

Your story does not
need an audience first.

An elder who held a community together. A parent who taught the letters. Someone returning to faith. You do not have to be well known to have something worth sharing.

Time to decide what you share.

One remote, recorded conversation. The questions arrive in advance. You receive the recording and can decline publication.

A moment with the sources

Let gentleness shape the conversation.

Sahih Muslim 2594a

From Prophet Muhammad ﷺ

Sahih · Authentic narration

إِنَّ الرِّفْقَ لاَ يَكُونُ فِي شَىْءٍ إِلاَّ زَانَهُ وَلاَ يُنْزَعُ مِنْ شَىْءٍ إِلاَّ شَانَهُ

Meaning in brief · Original English paraphrase · Inheriting IslamThe Prophet Muhammad ﷺ teaches that gentleness makes things beautiful, while its absence leaves them marred.

A reflection from the houseA conversation needs room for pauses, different memories, and what someone chooses not to share. Listening is part of the work.

Read the source, context, and text notes

Arabic text: Complete prophetic saying; transmission chain omitted.

Narrated by Aisha, wife of the Prophet ﷺ.

Aisha narrates this saying in the chapter on the virtue of gentleness. This record uses narration 2594a alone; it does not combine details from neighboring narrations.

The English above is a brief paraphrase, not a word-for-word translation. The reflection is our editorial writing.
