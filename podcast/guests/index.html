03.1 / An invitation

A life of faith.
In your own words.

An invitation to talk about the faith you received, how you live it, and what you hope to pass on. Your public profile is not what makes your experience worth hearing.

Put yourself forward Recommend someone else

Season one · not launched This is an invitation to introduce yourself, not a confirmed recording.

Remote conversationUsually one to two hours, with breaks when needed.

Questions in advanceRead the three themes before deciding.

About the surah & verse numbering

A surah is a chapter of the Qur’an; an ayah is a numbered verse. These totals follow the Kufan numbering used by Quran.com. Other traditional verse-counting systems exist.

وَقُل لِّعِبَادِي يَقُولُواْ ٱلَّتِي هِيَ أَحۡسَنُۚ

Opening clause of the verse

Meaning in brief · Original English paraphraseAllah instructs His servants to choose the best manner of speech.

A reflection from the house

Speak from your own experience and leave space for what others choose to keep private. A thoughtful conversation does not need every detail.

Context & a step to carry with you

The full verse warns that Satan stirs discord between people and is an enemy to humankind. The instruction about speech is connected to that warning.

One step to carry with you

Choose what you are comfortable sharing before you offer your story.

Who we are looking for

The people who help faith take root.

The inheritance also moves through people who have never stood on a stage. A public profile is not a requirement.

  • An elder who held a community together through a decade nobody wrote down
  • A revert twenty years in — long enough that the story is not about the shahādah anymore
  • An imam who has married and buried three generations of the same families
  • A mother who taught six children to read before anyone thought to call it teaching
  • Anyone who was handed something and has thought about what happens to it next

The questions, in advance

The three questions, before we meet.

These three themes guide the conversation. We send them in advance so you have time to think, and you decide what you are comfortable sharing.

  1. 01

    What was handed to you

    The people who helped you learn, whether in childhood or after you came to Islam later. What they shared with you, and what you understood with time.

  2. 02

    How you carry it now

    The ordinary week, not the highlights. What it costs you. What you have had to work out for yourself as your circumstances changed.

  3. 03

    What you are handing forward

    To your children, your students, your community. What you are deliberately trying to pass on, what you hope others will carry with them, and what you would say to somebody starting where you started.

How long, and what you need

One conversation, remote, in one sitting — usually somewhere between an hour and two, with no rush and no producer counting minutes. If it needs a break, we take one.

You need nothing but a quiet room and a link we send you. No app to install, no account to make, no microphone to buy. It opens in whatever browser you already use, and if it does not work we will get on the phone and sort it out before we start.

What we will not do

We will not edit you into saying something you did not say. We will not publish an episode you are unhappy with. We will not ask you to comment on anything happening in the news, or on anybody else.

You get the recording, and you get to say no after the fact. If you sit down, talk for two hours and then decide you would rather it did not go out, that is the end of it and there will be no argument about it.

What you get back

A record of it, that belongs to you too.

Every conversation gets its own page here with the whole thing written out — not a summary and not a highlight reel. A transcript can be read, searched, and returned to by people who would rather read than listen.

You get the audio and the video files. Use them however you like. We are not asking for exclusivity over your own words.

Why we record video, even at the start

Season one publishes as audio. We record video anyway, and keep it.

Video preserves more of the conversation: the pauses, expressions and gestures alongside the voice. You receive the files and can decide whether you are comfortable with publication.

If your community should hear it

The people most likely to listen to your episode are the people who already know you. Every episode page is built to be forwarded — your name, your masjid if you want it named, and one line worth sending on its own.

More often, it is somebody else

Someone you would like us to meet.

You may know someone who would never put themselves forward: an elder, a parent, a teacher, or a friend whose experience has stayed with you. Share the invitation with them and ask whether they would welcome an introduction.

If that is you, ask them first. Share this page or the PDF with them before passing on personal details. With their permission, tell us who they are and whether we may mention your name. If they say no, that is completely fine and nobody will be pestered.

What helps most in a recommendation

Their name, roughly where they are, and — the important part — one thing you have heard them say that made you think of this. One sentence is enough. It is the difference between a cold email and one that opens with something true about them.

Season one is being prepared

Nothing has been published yet, and no release date has been announced. An introduction starts a conversation; it is not a commitment to record.

If you are not sure you have a story

Almost nobody thinks they do. The conversation is not about achievements — it is about what you were given and what you are trying to pass on, and everybody who was handed this deen has an answer to that.

Write anyway and we will work out on a call whether it is a fit. There is no obligation either way.
